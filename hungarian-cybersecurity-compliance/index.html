Class exercise · one company at a time

Work out what to check

Read the fictional company story on screen. Enter only facts it gives you, then make a hypothesis before you reveal the guidance. “The story does not say” is a useful answer.

Step 1 of 4

What is the company’s business domain?

Choose every activity the story describes. These are everyday descriptions, not legal categories.

What does it do?
Use the story, not a company lookup. Do not infer a service just because it sounds likely from the company name.

Who owns it, and how large is it?

Use the figures and ownership facts in the story. Do not decide legal size from the word “small” alone.

Figures that may change a size-based route

For some listed services, the law looks at the entity’s legal size, staff and financial statements. Group links can change that assessment.

Use a documented company/group assessment if the story provides one. Do not calculate this legal size category from the short profile.

The financial test generally needs annual revenue/budget above €10 million and, where the company must prepare the relevant statements, balance-sheet total above €10 million. Do not estimate missing figures: use the company/group data and the official exchange rate at the relevant year-end.

What data, products, customers or official decisions are mentioned?

A customer or supplier can create contract duties without automatically giving the company the customer’s direct legal duties.
